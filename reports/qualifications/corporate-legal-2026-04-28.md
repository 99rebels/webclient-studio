**Website:** https://www.corporatelegal.ie
**Data Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Company Overview

Corporate Legal is a Dublin-based commercial barristers practice specialising in Irish company and commercial law. The practice is led by Mahmud Samad BL and offers direct access — meaning clients (including foreign businesses) can engage without going through a solicitor first. Their service areas cover a wide range of corporate law matters: company restoration, shareholder disputes, director duties, liquidation advice, receivers, examinership, partnership law, debt recovery, and SaaS agreements. They offer fixed-fee applications, which suggests a pricing model aimed at transparency and accessibility.

The practice positions itself as serving both domestic Irish clients and foreign lawyers/businesses who need legal guidance in Ireland. Their site has an international page and a dedicated page for foreign business services, indicating cross-border work is a meaningful part of their practice.

## Current Web Presence

**Website:** https://www.corporatelegal.ie
**Platform:** Static HTML (no CMS detected — hand-built or very old system)
**Quality Assessment:** Dated. The site uses plain .html URL extensions, has no meta description on the homepage, no detected CMS, and no social media presence beyond Facebook. The content is extensive (57 pages covering many practice areas in detail) but the technical foundation is clearly old. No modern frameworks, no blog with recent updates, no visible SEO optimisation.

Key observations:
- No meta description on the homepage (basic SEO miss)
- No CMS — every page is a static .html file, meaning content updates require manual HTML editing
- Only social link found: Facebook (no LinkedIn, which is notable for a professional services firm)
- The site has depth of content but the presentation likely undermines credibility for prospective clients comparing against modern law firm websites
- A contact form, phone number, and email are readily available — the functional basics work

## Fit Assessment

**Score:** 6/10
**Verdict:** MODERATE

**Reasoning:**
- **Need Signal (Medium-High):** The site is clearly outdated — static HTML, no CMS, no meta tags, minimal social presence. For a practice that markets itself to both domestic and international clients, the website is not doing them any favours. A modern, professional site would be a significant upgrade.
- **Size Fit (High):** Appears to be a solo or very small practice. Exactly the right size for a freelance web designer. Not enterprise, not too small.
- **Budget Signal (Medium):** Barristers charge professional fees and this practice serves commercial/corporate clients. They're not a startup operating on fumes. However, their fixed-fee model suggests cost-consciousness — they may be sensitive about spend. Budget likely exists but needs to be justified.
- **Accessibility (High):** Dublin-based, same timezone. Phone, email, and contact form all readily available. The direct-access model of their practice suggests they're open to direct outreach. No gatekeeper problem.
- **Timing Signal (Low):** No indication they're actively looking for web services. The site has been this way for a while — they may not see it as a priority or may not know what a modern site could do for them.

## Key Findings

- The site has 57 pages of detailed legal content — a rebuild would need to preserve or migrate all of this, not start from scratch
- No CMS means the client likely updates content manually (or doesn't update it at all) — a CMS would be a clear value-add
- Missing LinkedIn presence is unusual for a professional services firm marketing to corporate clients
- The practice serves an international audience — a multilingual or at least internationally-facing design would matter
- Testimonials exist on the site but are self-hosted only — no Google Business profile or third-party review presence was found
- The practice areas are quite niche (Irish company law) — content expertise would need to be handled by the client, not fabricated

## Recommendation

Worth a careful approach. The technical need is real — the site is objectively outdated and that undermines their positioning as "Ireland's leading commercial barristers." But legal professionals often rely on referrals over web presence, so the perceived urgency may be low. The pitch should lead with credibility and client acquisition: a modern site would convert the international traffic they're clearly trying to attract (they have a dedicated international services page) and strengthen their position against better-presented competitors. Don't lead with "your site looks old" — lead with "your content is strong but the presentation isn't doing it justice."
